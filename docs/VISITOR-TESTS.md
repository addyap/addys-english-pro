# Watching real visitors use the site

Analytics say *what* happens; watching five people use the site says *why*.
Five short sessions find most of the problems that matter. Run them once now,
and again after any big change.

## Who to ask

Aim for five to seven people who have **not** worked on the site and don't
know what you want them to find:

- 2–3 **training buyers**: an HR or training manager, or a manager who would
  book English training for a team; one individual professional.
- 2–3 **small-business owners** who might want a website: a restaurant,
  holiday let, shop or tradesperson, ideally with foreign customers.
- 1 **current or past learner**, for the practice tools.

Friends of friends are fine; close friends and family are too kind.

## Set-up

- 20–30 minutes each, in person or by video with screen sharing.
- Half on a **phone**, half on a computer: most visitors arrive on a phone.
- Start on the homepage (`https://www.antonyaddy.com`), in a private window.
- Ask them to **think aloud**. Take notes; record only with their permission.

## What to say at the start (read it as written)

> Thank you for helping. I'm testing the website, not you; there are no wrong
> answers, and anything that confuses you is useful to me. Please say what
> you're thinking as you go: what you're looking at, what you expect, what
> surprises you. I may not answer questions while you're doing the tasks,
> because I want to see how the site works on its own.

Then **say as little as possible**. If they're stuck for more than a minute,
ask "What would you do now?", not "Try the menu."

## Tasks

Give one task at a time, written on a card or in the chat. Use the ones that
fit the person.

1. **First impression (everyone).** Look at this page for ten seconds, then
   tell me: who is this person, what do they offer, and who is it for?
2. **Training buyer.** Your team has to run meetings with an English-speaking
   client from next quarter. Find out whether this could help, and what it
   would involve. Stop when you'd know whether to get in touch.
3. **Training buyer.** You want to know roughly how a training would start
   and how it's paid for. Find that out.
4. **Business owner.** You run a business with foreign customers and your
   website is only in French. Find out whether this person could help and
   what the first step would be.
5. **Business owner.** Find an example of a site this person has built for a
   business like yours.
6. **Everyone.** Start an enquiry about what interests you, up to the point
   just before sending. (Tell them not to send it.)
7. **Learner.** You want to practise speaking English between lessons. Find a
   tool that could help, and say whether you'd need an account or a
   microphone.

## What to watch for

For each task, note:

- **Did they finish?** Yes / with help / no.
- **Where did they hesitate** or go back? Which words did they misread?
- **What did they expect** that wasn't there (prices, examples in their
  sector, availability, a phone number, …)?
- **What did they say out loud** that surprised you? Write their exact words.

## Questions at the end

- In one sentence, what does Antony do?
- Was anything confusing or missing?
- Would you get in touch? What would make you more (or less) likely to?
- If you'd recommend this to someone, who would it be?

## Afterwards

Within a day, list every problem, then mark how many people hit it.
Fix first what **three or more** people struggled with; ignore one-off
opinions about colours or wording unless they stopped someone from finishing a
task. Add the date and the main findings below.
